Your living room just invented a game.

Cadabra is a party game for Fire TV. Everyone throws ideas in from their phone, an AI designer turns them into a real physics game, and a few seconds later the room is playing it. Phones are the controllers. Nothing to install.

From a shout to a game in under a minute.

"Cadabra" was Amazon's first name. Abracadabra means I create as I speak, and that is the whole game: the room says what it wants, and the TV builds it.

Seven games. Four looks. Real physics.

Cadabra ships with seven built-in games, and every game the AI invents is built from the same parts. Each one runs on planck physics: bumpers kick, balls bounce, crates topple and barrels explode.

Any game, in any of four looks.

All the art is drawn in code, so a look is a setting, not an asset pack. The AI picks one for each pitch, with music and sound to match.

Don't like a rule? Change it from your phone.

After every round, the phones get ten seconds to ask for something different. The AI patches the game, shows what it changed, and you play the new version.

The AI designs. It never writes code.

A game is a GameSpec: data that composes nine tested blocks with a physics world and a look. The designer builds specs with tool calls, and a spec only reaches the TV after four bots finish a full round with it.

{{ARCH}}

The TV is in charge

A deterministic, seeded engine and the physics run on the stick. Phones only send inputs.

Bots test every game

The playtest gate says exactly what failed, like "only 1 of 4 bots scored", so the model fixes it in its next call.

Nobody waits for a model

Pitches get 8 s, the winner 25 s, tweaks 15 s. Each step has a fallback that is already good.

Family-safe by default

Bedrock Guardrails screens every idea, typed tweak, pitch and caption, whichever model answers.

Make any phone a controller.

We pulled the controller layer out into couchpad, an MIT library for TV and web party games. Scan a QR code and the phone is a gamepad.

  • Direct by default. A WebRTC DataChannel on the local network, 13 to 16 ms measured.
  • Relay when it has to. Falls back to a WebSocket relay with chunking, a reliable queue, keepalive and rejoin.
  • Widgets included. Joystick, button, hold, tilt, swipe, draw, text and vote.
  • Bring your own server. Ships with a reference signaling server and an example.
